01The vision

We are setting the stage for an enterprise-level system that lets you quarterback the enterprise. The vision is not a dashboard. It is an AI-native system that moves you from day-to-day operator to enterprise quarterback: knowing which lever to pull, when, and through whom.

02Where v1 starts

Get a live dashboard into your hands for the next quarterly executive meeting in October. v1 is a conversation catalyst, not a production system. Success is that it puts one shared picture of reality on the table, sparks the right exec-team discussion, surfaces what's missing, and earns the feedback that shapes v2. We optimize for speed-to-live and "tangible enough to react to" over completeness or automation.

03The problem v1 addresses

CENTURY 21 Affiliated runs roughly 80 offices, 2,000 agents, 12,000 transactions a year, ~$3.5B in sales and ~$100M GCI, plus a web of ancillary lines (title, mortgage, insurance, warranty) and outside services. Three realities make transparency, not features, the core problem:

  • Margin compression is the backdrop for everything. Company dollar has fallen from ~26–28% (2019) to ~14–18% today; net margin from ~8–10% to ~2–4%. The cause is volume, not rate — US home sales dropped from ~7.2M (2020) to ~4M/yr. Recovering profit now depends on levers and lines that are hard to see clearly.
  • The data exists, but it is siloed. The numbers live across Darwin, ShowingTime, Realitix, BrokerKit, eRelo, and bank spreadsheets, and across many people. Getting them means asking someone for a report or deciphering a bulk P&L — and the result is often stale by the time you see it.
  • The scorecard is broken. Three iterations of the EOS scorecard, none delivering the "deserted island" clarity you want: hand someone a single sheet and they understand how the company is running.

The cost of all three: too much time spent accessing reports and combing for the right information just to understand where the business is and what levers can be pulled. Accountability, consolidation, and investment calls all lag — keeping you stuck in operator mode.

04Who it's for

  • Primary (v1): Dan. The one-screen morning view.
  • Secondary (v2): the executive team and local office leaders. For the accountability drill-downs and an eventual EOS scorecard tie-in.

06What success looks like

For a discussion artifact, the reaction is the measure. These are the reactions we're designing for.

The anchor — the must-have
"I don't have to ask anyone for a report or wade through a P&L to find my number anymore. It's five minutes a day."

Measures the core change: v1 kills the ask-and-wait loop and the decode-the-bulk-report loop. Every number you want is self-serve, and the whole daily retrieval collapses to about five minutes.

"Holy shit, I never thought we had access to this type of data."

Credibility & access — it's believable, and it surfaces data that felt out of reach.

"Wait, how are we defining that number?"

Engaged discussion — the team pushes instead of nodding. Debating definitions means they find it real.

"Can I get a version of this for my region?"

It leverages into their teams — jumps off your desk into the org.

"The real gold would be drilling into a single office, or seeing our actual title capture rate."

Reveals the roadmap — the team defines v2 instead of us guessing it.

"I've been checking this every morning. Let's have the next version ready for Q4."

Stickiness — you pull the next version forward yourself.

08Scope boundaries

In v1

  • Modules A–F at company-level roll-up
  • Fed from documents you already receive
  • Ancillary included, but manual and static
  • The Cowork conversational layer (see §10)

Out of v1 — these seed the roadmap

  • Office and region drill-down
  • Live integrations and APIs via IT
  • Automated document capture
  • Historical data and trend analysis
  • L10 / EOS accountability in the roll-up
  • Real ancillary insight and timeliness
  • The partnership-transparency fix — a board and partner conversation you own, not a build
From the boards: the sticky-note pass sorted the business for us. Pink marked blockers (the ancillary partnerships) — kept out of the build. Yellow marked what you want but don't have (the brokerage view) — the target of v1. Blue marked nice-to-haves — parked. v1 is the yellow, at company level.

09How the data flows

A document-drop folder (or a tagged email) feeds an AI skill that reads the reports, maps them to the defined tiles, and renders the view. The first pass defines exactly which numbers; after that it's a repeatable pull.

10Talking to your data in Cowork

Alongside the dashboard, you get a conversational layer at no extra build. Your reports live in one folder inside Cowork, so you can simply talk to Claude about your own data. There's no chat box built into the dashboard and none planned — the dashboard stays the clean static view. This is just you opening Cowork with the folder connected and asking.

  • Ask questions of your numbers in plain English. "Why are closings down this month?" "Which lines made money last quarter?"
  • Get summaries and callouts. "What changed since last month?" "What should I be paying attention to this week?"
  • Compare and dig into the reports. Our numbers vs. the market, this month vs. last, one office or line vs. another.
  • Refresh and reshape. Drop in the latest reports and have the dashboard regenerated, or ask for a different cut.

Honest bounds. It works off what's in the folder — as current as the latest reports, and as deep as those reports go (company-level in v1). It's a way to talk to your data, not a live system.

11What we'll define together

A short list of decisions we close in the first working pass — most of them are the exact metric definitions:

  • Exact metric definitions and math per tile (pipeline ratios, "expense per agent," the market-share basis).
  • Which ancillary lines make the v1 manual snapshot, and at what grain.
  • Delivery surface: a standalone file vs. a page on dancruz.com.
  • The train-and-grow-agents metric: define for v1, or defer to v2.

Building even v1 means pulling reports your team owns, so this isn't a solo build. Said plainly up front: I'll need to talk directly with a few people to get the source material, and I'll need you to clear the way and confirm what's shareable.
