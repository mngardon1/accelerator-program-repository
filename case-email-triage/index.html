How I gave Claude my inbox.

The Problem

Three inboxes, three places to hate

I hate email. And I have three inboxes, which means three places to hate it.

Most of it is noise. The two or three things that actually need me are buried in the middle.

The Solution

I handed the sorting to Claude

Claude reads my inboxes, sorts every email into a labeled queue, and drafts replies to the easy ones in my voice. By the time I open my inbox, the noise is filed and what's left is the short list that actually needs me.

It drafts. It never sends. Nothing leaves my name without my approval.

How It Works

I told it how I think about email

The magic isn't the AI. It's that I told Claude, in plain English, how I think about my own email. Once that's written down, it applies my judgment to every message.

What I wanted

  • Every email sorted before I open the inbox
  • Only the few things that truly need me, in one place
  • Easy replies drafted in my voice, never sent without my yes
  • The whole thing running on its own, no button to push

Then what I set up. The core is a set of labeled queues, borrowed from Dan Martell, who hasn't read his own inbox since 2022 because an assistant files everything into queues first. I handed that job to Claude instead of a person. The moment an email gets a label, it leaves the inbox, so my inbox only holds what I haven't decided on yet.

  • Escalate to meTime-sensitive, a decision only I can make, money, legal, or a real problem. The folder I open first.
  • To respondEasy replies, drafted for me.
  • ReviewI need to see it, no reply.
  • Waiting on someoneThe ball is in their court.
  • Receipts and financialPassive money, auto-renewals, confirmations.
  • ReadingNewsletters and long-form.
  • RequestsInbound asks like intros and podcast invites.
  • UnsureGenuine judgment calls, flagged instead of guessed.
  • NotificationsSocial and platform noise, batched and cleared.

I built those rules by talking. "Unpaid invoices come to me." "This newsletter is just noise." Each correction became a permanent rule Claude reads every time it runs. I never touched code. I described how I decide, and the system started deciding that way.

The Feedback Loop

It learns every time it's wrong

Correcting it is a single sentence. When it miscategorizes something, I tell it in a normal chat: "Anything from Stripe goes to Receipts." "Emails from Sarah are always Escalate." "Stop drafting replies to newsletters." Claude updates the rulebook it reads on every run, so the fix is permanent, not a one-off. The next pass already knows.

No settings screen. No retraining. I talk, the rules change, and the system gets more like me over time.

The Result

Five, then thirty, then 1,435

I didn't hand it everything on day one. I gave it five emails, just to see if I trusted it. It labeled them, filed them, and drafted nothing, because nothing needed a reply. Right call.

Then a bigger test: thirty emails. It sorted them, drafted the easy replies, and flagged the two that needed me. Still right.

So I let it loose on the backlog: 1,435 unread. One pass took it down to a handful that actually needed a decision. In a single batch it cleared 183 messages down to 10.

The old way

  • Open three inboxes every morning
  • Scan hundreds of unread by hand
  • Decide every single one myself
  • The important stuff stays buried

What I do now

  • Inbox sorted before I look
  • A short list that actually needs me
  • Easy replies already drafted
  • The noise is gone before I see it
I got back the worst part of my morning. And I built the whole thing by talking.

Under The Hood

What Claude built in the background

I don't code. I described what I wanted, and Claude built it. The whole timeline:

01 · Connect the inboxes

A login script (auth.py) signs into each account once and saves the permission, so it never has to ask again. My part: sign in, click approve.

02 · Write the rules

Everything about how I think about email went into one plain-English rulebook (SYSTEM_PROMPT.md): the 10 labels, what escalates to me, and my reply voice. My part: talk it through.

03 · Build the two-pass engine

Claude wrote two Python scripts that do the work:

  • classifier.py — the fast pass. Rules-based, clears the obvious bulk (newsletters, receipts, notifications) in seconds.
  • gmail_helper.py — the smart pass. Reads the judgment calls, applies the right label, and drafts replies in my voice for the easy ones.

04 · Put it on a schedule

Claude set it to run automatically twice a day, plus a one-word command to run it on demand. My part: nothing. It just runs.

My total hands-on work: signing into my inboxes, creating the labels once, and giving corrections in plain sentences. Describing the system was my job. Building it was Claude's.
