Installing the Chief Method

To AI, you are just files

All we're doing here is turning your knowledge and taste into written files, readable to an AI Chief of Staff. You start with one folder, the AI writes the files, and you add to it over time. Each iteration, the system gets better.

6 steps · ~20 min · mostly talking

The Steps

Step 1 · The frame

A Chief of Staff is only helpful if it knows you and what you want

You're setting up a learning "vault," all about you: how you work, what's on your plate, your plans and rules for getting things done. The Chief updates it for you and keeps you on task. This is exactly what you'd expect a human chief of staff to do, except a machine needs to read it, literally.

When done right, AI stops being generic and starts being YOURS.
A quick prime before you start

If you have an assistant, or any kind of organizational system, think about what gets written down for it: SOPs, reference information, how you like things done, the decisions already made. What's important there? That's the kind of foundational information we're going to build into your Chief of Staff.

Step 2 · The vault

One folder is your whole system

You make one folder, name it Chief, and connect it to a Cowork Project. That folder is the vault: the one place your Chief reads from and writes to, every session. Connecting it as a Project makes it stick, so you never have to wire it up again.

  1. Your computerMake a new folder and name it Chief. Putting it in your Documents folder is safest. (Want it to reach your phone? Put it in Dropbox / OneDrive / iCloud, then set that folder to "Always keep on this device.")
  2. AppOpen the Claude desktop app. In the left sidebar, click Cowork.
  3. AppClick New project, name it Chief, and create it.
  4. AppInside the project, click Connect a folder. A window opens to browse your computer. Click through to the Chief folder you just made, select it, and click Open.
  5. AppIf Claude asks permission to use the folder, click Allow. That folder is now your vault. Everything your Chief writes lands here, and you can open it any time.
On ChatGPT or Copilot?

The equivalent is a ChatGPT Project (or Custom GPT) with knowledge files, or a Copilot Agent. One catch: those read files you upload but don't write back to a folder that grows. You'd re-upload. Cowork (and Claude Code) are what make the vault self-maintaining.

Setting up on one of those? Follow the steps for ChatGPT or Copilot.

On claude.ai (web) instead of the desktop app?

Web and mobile can't read or write a local folder. You can still do all of this, but you'll copy-paste the files it generates and save them yourself. The self-writing loop (Step 5) needs the Cowork desktop app.

Step 3 · Build it

Paste one prompt. Then just talk.

Paste the master Chief prompt below as your first message in the project, then say "Let's build my Chief." That prompt turns Claude into your Chief: it knows how to interview you and write your files, so you never open a text editor. Your whole foundation gets built from one conversation.

It offers two ways to go: Dump (paste a bio, deck, or notes and it organizes them) or Guided (one question at a time). Either way, you watch it capture as you talk, and it ends by drafting the account-instructions text you'll use in the next step.

Step 4 · Your account settings

    
The part that reaches your phone

    
Paste the block your Chief drafted in the last step into a setting called Instructions for Claude. The vault only works on this computer, but this setting travels with your login, to every device you're signed into, including your phone. So any Claude chat sounds like you, even on your phone where there's no folder.

Keep it lean

While you're in Settings, a few worth knowing

    
You won't touch everything. These are the big ones.

    
    
      
  • Memory. Claude can automatically remember useful things across all your chats (your work, your tools, recurring context) and pull them into future conversations. It's on by default. Your vault already does this on purpose, in files you own and can see; Memory is the automatic, behind-the-scenes version of the same idea. Leave it on for passive convenience everywhere, or turn it off if you'd rather all your context live in the vault.
    • 
      
  • Privacy and model training. There's a setting for whether your chats are used to improve Claude's models. Worth a conscious choice. Set it how you want it.
    • 
      
  • Connectors. You can connect Gmail, Google Drive, Calendar, and more. This is how your Chief will later read your world and take action, so it's good to know it's there.
    • 
      
  • Model and Extended thinking. You can pick a stronger model, and turn on deeper reasoning for harder problems.
    • 
      
  • Temporary chat. A one-off chat Claude won't save or remember. Good for anything sensitive.

Step 5 · The rhythm

    
Three moves, and it gets smarter

    
Run your day with three commands. This is the everyday loop: how your vault grows and how your Chief learns your rules. The payoff is compounding. Correct it once and it sticks, plans persist, and nothing evaporates.

    

    

      
Capture: X
Drops a task or note into your inbox the moment it hits you.

      
What's next?
Your Chief reads your plan and gives the single most important next move.

      
Wrap
Updates your plan, clears the inbox, and asks what to remember before you close out.

    

    
The one habit that compounds: when it gets something wrong, say "remember that." It writes the rule down and follows it from then on. Correct once, and it sticks.

  


  
  

    
Step 6 · You're live

    
Your Chief is running

    
That's the foundation. Your Chief reads your vault every session, so you never start from zero, and it grows as you work.

    
This is not a black box

      
Everything your Chief does is in your control. Every file is yours to open, read, and change. You didn't install a product that runs one fixed way. As you get comfortable, you build on it: your own commands, your own skills, your own workflows and routines. The Chief doesn't box you in. It just gets better at capturing where you are, keeping you on task, and being a real partner.

    

    
A few things that make it feel effortless:

    
    
      
  • Let your Chief act in your browser. Install the Claude in Chrome extension (search "Claude in Chrome" in the Chrome Web Store, add it, then sign in and pin it). It works on any paid plan and lets your Chief navigate, click, and fill forms on your behalf, not just talk. It's in beta, so glance at what it's doing before you let it click.
    • 
      
  • Talk, don't type. Use dictation (or a tool like Wispr Flow). The Chief is a conversation, and talking is faster than typing.
    • 
      
  • Screenshot everything. To give direction or feedback, grab a shot of what you're looking at (⌘+Ctrl+Shift+4 on Mac) and paste it in.
    • 
      
  • Browse your vault in Obsidian. Point Obsidian at your Chief folder to read and search your files in a nicer view.
    • 
    

    
What's next

    
Once the loop feels natural, connect a tool (email, calendar) or have your Chief build its first repeatable process. That's where the leverage compounds, but the foundation you just built is the thing everything else stands on.
