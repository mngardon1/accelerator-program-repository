The Ask

"Most of what we do is just how we've always done it"

The owner of a financial advisory practice brought this to a scoping session. Here it is, as he wrote it:

"I want to be able to take a look at the processes and ways we currently manage our financial advisory practice. Most of what we do is just because that's how we've always done it. I'd like to be able to spot inefficiencies, and come up with better ways to manage our office. I think we're trying to raise quality, and that means proposing processes or ways of working that we haven't done before. This working in 90 days: Claude has a high-level view of our systems and processes. I can bring in a specific workflow, point to the SOPs or process docs we currently have and explore ways we could re-engineer them. That doesn't just mean tech, but AI or automation can definitely help. Single result: redesign at least one specific process that saves over $100,000 in value."

It's a good ask. It's also not a plan yet.

What Scoping Did

One paragraph filled the goal. The rest had holes.

AI sorted the paragraph into a scope doc, section by section. Each section was marked filled, partly filled or still open.

Filled

The outcome

Find better ways to run the office than "how we've always done it." Raise quality. Win: redesign one process worth $100,000 or more.

Filled

The 90-day picture

AI holds a high-level view of the practice. He brings one workflow and its process docs, and they redesign it together.

Partly

The inputs

Some process docs exist. Nobody knows which processes are written down and which live in people's heads.

First job: gather the docs into one place, and list which processes have none.

Partly

The output

A redesigned process, plus a living map of how the practice works. The format, and who reads it, weren't stated.

Open

How the work runs today

The actual processes, who runs them and where the time goes. None of it was in the paragraph.

Question to close it: "Walk me through one process start to finish, the way it really happens today."

Open

His standard for "better"

Without it, AI proposes redesigns he wouldn't sign off on.

Question to close it: "When you see a process that works well, what's true about it?"

Open

Compliance and trust

Financial advice is regulated. Record-keeping rules, client confidentiality and how accurate a redesign must be had to be settled before any build.

The Insight

The bottleneck was the paperwork, not the AI

The hard part was getting the process docs in one place and knowing which ones existed at all.

That's what scoping is for. It shows you the real first step before you spend a dollar on the exciting one.

The Plan

Smallest useful version first

  1. 20-minute inventory. List every process bucket in the practice. No detail yet.
  2. Pick one. Choose the process most likely to clear the $100,000 bar.
  3. Document today. Write down how it really works now.
  4. Redesign it. Propose the new version, with AI and without.
  5. Ship and measure. Put it in place, measure the saving, then move to the next process.

Without scoping

  • Start building on a paragraph
  • Find the compliance wall halfway in
  • AI guesses at what "better" means
  • No way to tell if you hit $100,000

With scoping

  • Goal and win written down
  • Gaps named, with the question that closes each
  • Real bottleneck found on day one
  • A five-step plan, smallest step first

Scoping is the conversation that closes the gaps before anyone builds.
